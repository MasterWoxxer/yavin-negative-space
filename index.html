Why

Many studies and experiments are run and never published, for reasons nobody knows. Most likely the data disproved, or was inconclusive about, the claim or theory that drove the work; sometimes it supports real conclusions different from the hypothesis. One item may say little. In aggregate, it is highly valuable to science. Negative Space publishes it.

How it works

  1. Send what you have.Relatively raw documents are fine, with authorship and attribution where relevant.
  2. We check that it's checkable.Methods recorded, the data if there is any, who ran it and when. That's the only bar. There is no full peer review.
  3. It's published and indexed.Each item goes into existing scholarly indexing, so the public can find it.
  4. It's open to annotation.Reviewers on Transparent Peer Review can annotate it afterwards. Optional, never a gate.

What we publish

Null results

Studies that found no effect.

Inconclusive results

Below significance, underpowered, or mixed.

Failed replications

Attempts to repeat a published finding that didn't hold.

Abandoned work

Projects that stopped, with whatever methods and data exist.

Methods Integrity Panel

Every item carries a panel describing its methods: preregistration, sample size, power analysis, effect sizes, deviations from protocol. It describes and indexes the work, so readers can search by method. Missing fields are shown as missing, never a reason to reject.
